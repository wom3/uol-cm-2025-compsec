Question 1
In real-world cybersecurity practice, it’s not enough to design secure technical solutions, you must also be able to explain incidents and responses clearly to non-technical stakeholders (e.g., executives, board members or partners). This task develops your ability to translate complex security issues into concise, accessible communication.

Write a short executive summary (100–150 words) that explains to a non-technical stakeholder:

the breach: what happened, in plain language
the redesign: the key change(s) made to secure the system
next steps: what actions will be taken to strengthen security further.
Guidance

Use clear, non-technical language (avoid jargon).
Focus on the business impact and reassurance (what’s being done, why it matters).
Keep it concise: a maximum of 150 words.

An unauthorised user was able to access parts of our system by exploiting a weakness in how access was controlled. This meant they could potentially view or alter sensitive information, creating a risk to customer trust and operations. To address this, we redesigned the system so that only authorised users can access critical data, removed the vulnerable access path, improved monitoring, and introduced stronger controls around passwords and system updates. We are also reviewing access permissions, training staff, and running regular security checks to detect issues earlier. These steps reduce the chance of a repeat incident and help protect our business, customers, and partners.
